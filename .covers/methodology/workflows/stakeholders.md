# Stakeholder review of defects and invariants

SPEC-TOOL-015/R2: preserved workflow guidance, extracted from the former full operating contract. Read the short COVERS.md contract first; higher-priority authority is unchanged. Paths in this reference are repository-root paths in the source distribution; prefix .covers/ after adoption unless already specified.

## Stakeholder review of defects and invariants

### Consolidated findings checkpoint (standard workflow)

This checkpoint is domain- and language-neutral: apply it to services, libraries, CLIs, data pipelines, desktop/mobile/web applications and mixed systems. Adapt categories and accountable roles to the actual project; a library may have consumers/maintainers rather than a business product owner. Reuse existing issue trackers or review documents where suitable, with stable references and decision provenance. Mark inapplicable categories explicitly rather than inventing findings. No fixed finding count, test framework, container platform, wiki or meeting format is required.

After bounded discovery and baseline/characterization evidence, and before selecting behavior-changing repair specs, present the whole known in-scope findings inventory together. Do not lead stakeholders through one defect per conversation. For large systems use one top-level findings index and manageable domain batches; preserve visibility of unresolved items across batches. Do not wait for an exhaustive audit, a working runtime when static evidence is sufficient, or 80% coverage before reviewing useful findings.

Use a single review packet with stable finding IDs, plain-language impact, evidence status (reproduced, source-supported, hypothesis or advisory match), primary source/revision, proposed next action and actual decision/priority/owner (pending where unknown). Keep quality gaps distinct from application defects. Add the exact related candidate invariant statements and scope for review. The stakeholder-review template is the decision worksheet; a wiki findings overview, when a wiki exists, is derived briefing with citations and links to canonical registers. No wiki or extra service is required. Keep security details access-appropriate and only sanitized summaries in the shared inventory; preserve one authoritative live remediation status per finding.

Collect fix / investigate / defer / not-a-defect selections by ID, or an explicit enumerated batch with exceptions, rationale, priority, owner and revisit triggers. Business dispositions remain separate from evidence confidence. Do not turn an untested hypothesis into a reproduced defect or vague positive feedback into approval of every item. Review candidate rules as approve / revise / reject / pending with exact version, actual reviewer role/authority, date and exercise/operational mode. Product priorities, engineering feasibility and security-risk acceptance retain their respective owners.

Reconcile actual decisions into the authoritative registers, specs and invariant catalog before affected implementation; then update the derived wiki. Selected fixes still require bounded repair specs, execution authority and meaningful regression red/green evidence. New discoveries enter the next batch; material changes re-open affected decisions only. No status is approved merely because this packet was prepared or reviewed by an agent. This is a standard human checkpoint, not an approval mechanism enforced by the CLI.

After recording discovered defects and candidate rules for a bounded domain or change, hold a lightweight stakeholder decision checkpoint before affected business-behavior repairs. Review actual requirements and observed evidence with the accountable business/product/domain owner: is each observation a confirmed defect, expected behavior, an accepted limitation or still unclear? Separate reproduction confidence from business disposition, and priority/fix-or-defer decisions from both. Do not imply all possible bugs have been discovered or require an exhaustive system audit before incremental review.

Then review candidate invariants with the appropriate domain, security or technical owner. Present the exact statement/version, scope/preconditions, severity, related defects/conflicts, exceptions and proposed enforcement/behavioral checks. Record approve, revise, reject or pending. A rule may be approved even though current code violates it; acceptance and passing/failing/unverified evidence are separate. Candidate rules remain candidates until real approval is recorded. No named accountable owner, silence, test success or an AI self-critique score is approval.

Use a concise separate Markdown record at specs/reviews/<domain>-stakeholder-review.md; start with scaffolding/stakeholder-review.md (or .covers/scaffolding/stakeholder-review.md after adoption). Record actual reviewer/role/authority, date, exact reviewed artifact version, decision/rationale, scoped exceptions and durable evidence reference. A conversation reply, authorized issue/document decision or reviewed local record can suffice; no new service, connector or formal meeting is mandatory. Never fabricate approval or contact stakeholders without permission. Keep private source content access-appropriate.

The review record links to authoritative spec/invariant IDs and versioned statements rather than creating competing live definitions. Reconcile approved decisions into governing specs and invariants.json, including meaningful enforcement/check mappings; refresh affected registry metadata. Keep approval evidence separate from behavioral verification. Preserve rejected/deferred decisions and earlier approvals; material meaning/version changes need re-review and explicit supersession. An accepted limitation cannot silently negate an applicable invariant. Current CLI checks do not validate reviewer authority or prove this human checkpoint occurred.

Unresolved business decisions block the affected behavior change, not all independent work. Unambiguous compiler/build repairs may proceed under their own technical specs; technical invariants still require the accountable technical owner. Urgent safety containment needs explicit authority and a recorded scope, not an agent-invented exception. Stakeholder agreement is not execution, commit or deployment permission. Keep the review proportional to the intended use; the user can be the accountable stakeholder for a solo project, but their decisions still must be explicit.

Decision evidence must preserve retrievable immutable reviewed meaning, not just a hash or mutable URL. Retain a commit/blob, diff against an identified base with any untracked content, access-appropriate snapshot/excerpt, or durable document version covering the statement, scope, preconditions and exceptions. Hashes verify the retained content. Apply this to security-risk decisions as well as invariant approvals, so later revisions cannot erase what was actually decided.

### Security findings and practice reviews

Keep a separate security findings register at specs/reviews/<domain>-security-findings.md using scaffolding/security-findings.md. Link stable finding, defect, invariant and spec IDs; each finding has one authoritative live status. Record evidence confidence separately from exposure, impact and severity; distinguish remediation, containment, deferred risk and verified closure. Business confirmation of a product bug is not security risk acceptance. Only the appropriately authorized risk owner can accept residual risk, with scope, rationale, controls and an expiry/review trigger. Do not lower the risk solely because current behavior is intentional. Keep sensitive details in access-controlled evidence storage, not a public repository; Markdown separation is not access control.

Review exercises may use one person in separately labeled business and developer roles. Record exercise/operational mode and acting role on each decision. Business decides intended behavior and priorities; developer assesses feasibility, technical evidence and implementation; an authorized security/risk owner decides security acceptance. Practice decisions are not production approvals and cannot silently promote candidate invariants to accepted. Operational approval requires a fresh explicit authorized decision; required independent review cannot be simulated by changing hats.
